01 / CHANGE THE PROCESS

A little chemistry.
A different color story.

Dark Melon simulates variations in the processing sensitivity of film’s dye layers, then the process of scanning that film back into a digital image. Randomizing those properties changes how colors respond to light—the sort of behavior that chemistry traditionally shaped.

The result can recall cross-processed film, expired stock, or simply the small unpredictabilities that made film different from a digital sensor. You’re exploring variations in a film-inspired process, rather than choosing from a fixed list of looks.

These are creative simulations, not measured matches to specific film stocks.

Keep exploring until something fits the photograph. The aim isn’t a single correct rendition; it’s an easy way to discover color relationships you might never have built by hand.

05 / COLOR BEYOND SDR

The look stays.
The highlights open up.

The ten individual photographs are exported in HDR. Dark Melon’s color system works with HDR throughout, so these process variations can carry into a rendition with brighter highlights.

Many conventional creative LUTs are designed around an SDR output. Here, the look is part of the development process, with no SDR-only LUT needed.

The original JPEGs and their HDR gain maps are preserved. HDR display depends on your browser, operating system, and screen; unsupported viewing paths show the SDR rendition. The nine-image contact sheet is a separate overview.
